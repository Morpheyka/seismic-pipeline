# 2. Related Work

Work on earthquake preparation and precursors documents incomplete physical models alongside many geophysical and geochemical observations [Rodkin2011; Cicerone2009; Conti2021]. Critical assessments emphasize that such observations do not establish operational forecasting [Geller1997; Conti2021]. Parallel biological threads describe animal behavioral anomalies before shocks [Kirschvink2000], possible environmental mediators [Grant2011], and occasional instrumented monitoring near event dates [Yokoi2003]. That literature supports looking for early biosignal indicators in a controlled setting, but it rarely supplies a statistical estimand for the *onset* of a physiological regime change on continuous recordings.

Stress and anxiety reshape rodent sleep, including paradoxical (REM) sleep, in a stressor-dependent way [Rampin1991; Meerlo1997; Palma2000; Sanford2003]. Spectral EEG/ECoG features make REM identifiable within a strong circadian structure [Fang2010; Vyazovskiy2005], and automated staging yields continuous hypnograms suitable for event-aligned analysis [Saevskiy2025]. How to time a regime onset under posterior uncertainty when samples are tiny and heterogeneous remains open.

Changepoint detection treats abrupt transitions in time-series dynamics as a core mining problem, with surveys spanning supervised and unsupervised families across monitoring domains [Aminikhanghahi2017]. Bayesian formulations provide retrospective hierarchical analyses [carlin1992hierarchical] and online run-length inference for regime shifts [Adams2007]. In short autocorrelated series [Hyndman2018], random validation splits can leak temporal structure [Bergmeir2012], so small-sample settings favor models that expose uncertainty [Sinkovec2022] and compare predictive fit via tools such as PSIS-LOO [Vehtari2017]. High-capacity anomaly detectors are less attractive when event–animal pairs are scarce and external seismic context is irregular rather than fully labeled.

Open-world anomaly detection stresses that normality, labels, and operating conditions may change after deployment [Chandola2009; Han2023]. Concept-drift surveys organize adaptation under non-stationary streams [Gama2014], and lifelong / continual anomaly detection asks models to retain past knowledge while absorbing new regimes [Faber2024]. Those lines motivate studying anomalies when the external context evolves and positive labels are rare. They do not require every case study to ship an online adaptation loop: a complementary need is interpretable regime-onset evidence on short, heterogeneous physiological windows under an irregular seismic driver.

This paper sits at that intersection. We cast eight-day event-aligned REM *mean* windows as a single-changepoint task for a seismic-event-associated anomaly in a brain-derived sleep signal, screening profile hyperparameters that yield interpretable \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) under Student-\(t\) or skew-normal likelihoods. Secondary range summaries use an interval-inflated Beta when needed; the main narrative remains mean-centered. The study does not implement continual normality-shift adaptation, does not claim operational earthquake prediction [Geller1997], and does not measure stress with independent biomarkers.
