# 7. Discussion and Limitations

Continuous ECoG-based hypnograms provide a brain-derived REM signal in which a single-changepoint model finds a coherent regime-shift anomaly near seismic events. That confirmation is statistical and instrumented: it does not rest on visual watching of animal behavior, and it does not equate REM change with a measured stress hormone response. Under mean features with Student-\(t\) or skew-normal likelihoods, posterior means \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) concentrate a few days from the event, and LOO ranks which \(N\), overlap, and mean likelihood describe that onset most coherently inside one builder. A Gaussian two-mean scan on the same discrete grid does not recover that mid-window consensus, so the detection claim is tied to the heavy-tailed mean model rather than to any two-regime partition.

Mean and range are not interchangeable summaries. Under mean Student-\(t\) the location bulk barely moves and the contrast lives in tails, scale, and \(\tau\); under IIB range the readable signal is a pre-onset ceiling near one that thins after \(\tau_{\mathrm{MAP}}\). Secondary range checks use IIB@$0.9$ because a plain Beta diverges at that boundary after min–max normalization. Confirmatory checks stay narrow: one shared-\(\nu\) cell, one \(\nu\)-split sibling, and one IIB range refit.

The estimand is an onset inside an eight-day window; larger \(\tau\) is closer to the shock. The within-window day-shuffle control, with synthetic power calibration and twenty-seed peak tallies (Fig.~\ref{fig:null-within-window}), shows that the mid-window onset consensus depends on event-aligned day order. That supports an anomaly in windows aligned to the shock; it does not by itself identify the earthquake as the physiological cause. Limits include the small cohort (\(n=34\)), the single-changepoint assumption, the absence of independent stress biomarkers, and the lack of an operational forecasting claim [Geller1997].
