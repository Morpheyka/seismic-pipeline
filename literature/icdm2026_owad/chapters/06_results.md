# 6. Results

After day-mask with \(K=6\), the primary cohort contains \(34\) windows. The interpretive center of the study is whether a regime-shift anomaly appears in brain-derived REM summaries near seismic events, and which mean features and profile hyperparameters describe that onset most coherently.

## Mean features: stable onset distributions

Mean-only configurations (\(n=84\)) yield a pooled \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\approx 5.78\) (median \(5.80\)); none collapse to the single-regime edge \(\tau=2\). On the event-aligned index this corresponds to an onset about three days from the event (\(9-\tau\)). Both Student-\(t\) (\(n=42\); median \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]=5.64\)) and skew-normal (\(n=42\); median \(6.25\)) give compact mid-window histograms (Fig.~\ref{fig:mean-only}). Skew-normal shifts the onset slightly closer to the event than Student-\(t\) (mean paired \(\Delta\mathbb{E}[\tau]\approx +0.46\) on matched cells), while remaining inside the same multi-day band. These distributions are the main empirical support for a statistical anomaly in the REM signal: the posterior mean onset sits a few days from the event rather than following the no-anomaly patterns of a single-regime edge collapse or a near-uniform scatter across the window.

Within the mean slice, LOO ranks profile geometry and likelihood choice. Table~\ref{tab:loo-topk} lists the top-10 mean-only cells by \(\overline{\mathrm{elpd}}/(F\cdot E')\): denser daily profiles with \(N=24\) and low overlap lead, with Student-\(t\) and skew-normal nearly tied at the top, and mid-window \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) throughout. Ranks 2 and 7 have \(\hat k_{\max}=1.08\) and \(1.03\), so those elpd values are used only as an ordinal ranking [Vehtari2024]. Rank 1 screening \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]=6.03\) differs from the confirmatory shared-\(\nu\) refit (\(\approx 5.74\)) because the MCMC budget is longer, not because the cell changed. Higher elpd among retained mean cells tends to accompany mid-window \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) rather than single-regime edge modes. On the confirmatory daily-mean windows (\(n=33\) after the incomplete-day drop used in that refit), a retrospective Gaussian two-mean scan on the same grid \(\mathcal{T}\) yields median \(\tau_{\mathrm{ML}}=5.0\), with only \(10/33\) onsets in \(\{6,7\}\) and \(6/33\) matching the Bayesian MAP \(\tau=7\). That scan is not BOCPD, PELT, or online anomaly detection; it shows that a shared-variance Gaussian partition does not recover the same mid-window consensus as the Student-\(t\) model.

See `tables/loo_topk_mean_only.md` / `latex/table_loo_topk.tex` for the compact top-10 table (features, lik, \(N\), ov, normalized elpd, \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\), max Pareto-\(\hat k\)).

## Confirmatory mean diagnostics

A long confirmatory Student-\(t\) refit on the top mean-only cell (`daily:mean`, \(N=24\), \(\mathrm{ov}=0\); Table~\ref{tab:loo-topk}, rank~1) sharpens what the grid-level \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) summaries leave implicit. Posterior median predictive densities with a \(90\%\) band separate the before/after regimes more clearly than a mean-parameter plug-in curve: the bulk of the day-to-day mean stays nearly fixed, while the anomaly shows up in the tails and in the onset posterior (MAP \(\tau=7\), \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\approx 5.74\) under shared \(\nu\)). Allowing separate degrees of freedom per regime on the same cell moves \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) only slightly (\(\approx 6.01\)) and leaves \(\mu\) almost unchanged, but shrinks the \(\sigma\)-gap and yields \(\nu_2\approx 13<\nu_1\approx 30\)—heavier tails after the MAP onset (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag-mean-nu}). That \(\nu\)-split is a confirmatory check, not part of the primary LOO ranking.

## Within-window day-shuffle control

To test whether the mid-window onset peak requires the observed order of days inside each event-aligned window, we permute finite day observations independently within each window on the primary confirmatory Student-\(t\) cell, leaving NaN masks in place. A synthetic shared-onset series with true \(\tau=6\) recovers a sharp peak at \(6\); the same within-window shuffle flattens that peak (MAD to uniform drops from about \(0.25\) to \(0.04\)--\(0.07\)), so the null has power under a known common changepoint and moves toward the near-uniform no-anomaly pattern. On the real primary arm, over twenty shuffle seeds the modal peak \(\tau\) leaves the mid-window band: counts concentrate at the prior edges (\(8\) seeds at \(\tau=2\), \(6\) at \(\tau=8\), \(3\) at \(\tau=3\), and only \(3/20\) at \(\tau=7\); Fig.~\ref{fig:null-within-window}). That is the other no-anomaly pattern from the Method section: with day order destroyed, the model often finds no useful split and parks mass at \(\tau=2\) or \(\tau=8\). Averaging a few seed-wise PMFs can look spuriously peaked; seed-wise peak tallies are the right summary. The mid-window onset on real dates is therefore sensitive to within-window day order in windows already aligned to the shock.

## Secondary checks

When a range feature is added under IIB, jointly active mean-and-range cells stay mid-window and place \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) in a similar multi-day band (IIB mean+range median about \(5.7\)). A confirmatory IIB range-only refit (\(N=12\), \(\mathrm{ov}=0\); observations scaled as \(y=\mathrm{range}/2\)) on the density-safe cohort (\(n=34\)) with \(\tau_{\mathrm{MAP}}=7\) (\(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\approx 6.88\)) makes the unit-interval pattern explicit (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag-iib}): before the MAP onset, median \(y\approx 0.75\) with \(21.4\%\) of day-observations at \(y\ge 0.9\) (and \(6.1\%\) pinned at the ceiling \(y=1\)); after, median \(y\approx 0.71\) with only \(11.8\%\) at \(y\ge 0.9\) (\(1.5\%\) at one). Event-wise, median(after)\(<\)median(before) in \(20/34\) windows. Mask-off and before-only confirmatory runs on \(n=34\) move \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) farther from the event, showing sensitivity to the observation builder. These checks bound interpretation; they are not the primary evidence story.
