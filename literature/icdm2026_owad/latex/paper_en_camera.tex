\begin{abstract}
We test whether an instrumented, brain-derived REM signal in rats shows a statistical regime-shift anomaly near seismic events, and which features and profile hyperparameters describe that anomaly most coherently. Eight-day event-aligned hypnogram windows (\(n=34\) after day-mask with \(K=6\)) are summarized by daily and half-day REM means under a Bayesian single-changepoint model with marginalized discrete onset \(\tau\). Student-\(t\) and skew-normal likelihoods for mean features yield stable \(\mathbb{E}[\tau]\) distributions concentrated a few days from the event, without pathological prior-edge pile-up. PSIS-LOO ranks retained profile geometries (\(N\), overlap) and mean likelihoods inside one observation builder. A within-window day-shuffle control on the primary mean arm breaks that mid-window onset consensus across seeds (peak \(\tau\) leaves \(\{6,7\}\) for prior-edge modes), matching a synthetic single-changepoint power check; causal calendar attribution remains weak. Range likelihood families known a priori to destroy onset interpretability are excluded from the main analysis. The result is statistical evidence of an anomaly in a neural sleep signal---not visual behavioral proof and not operational earthquake prediction.
\end{abstract}
